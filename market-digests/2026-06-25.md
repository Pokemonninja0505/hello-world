## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1 — The Fed's hawkish pivot is ahead of where the market (and especially rates) has priced it.** Warsh's first meeting as Chair produced a dot plot that jumped a full 0.4 points for the 2026 median, and today's PCE confirmed the inflation backdrop hasn't cooled. Yet the 10Y yield actually *fell* yesterday — because falling oil is doing the market's disinflation work for it, masking the Fed's own hawkish signal. This is an early-stage divergence, not a crowded trade: gold and copper are already reacting (both at multi-week/multi-month lows), but equities and the long end of the curve have not. The risk is that equities are treating the Micron print as the dominant signal this week while underpricing a Fed that just told you, in writing, it leans toward hiking. Small caps (Russell 2000) and other duration-sensitive assets are the most exposed if the bond market catches up.

**Theme 2 — The AI trade is bifurcating from a uniform re-rating into a stock-picker's market.** Three major chip-complex data points landed within 48 hours — Micron beat decisively, Qualcomm reframed its TAM higher, and Broadcom guided light and didn't raise its full-year AI number. That's dispersion, not consensus. This follows a three-day chip-sector rout (June 21–23) triggered by a BofA rate-hike note and an Asian market tumble — so the AI trade just survived its second scare in two weeks, but each survival is now coming with more internal differentiation between winners (memory/HBM, diversifying platforms) and laggards (custom silicon names tied tightly to hyperscaler capex pacing). This is neither early nor exhausted — it's maturing into a phase where single-name execution matters more than the macro AI narrative alone.
