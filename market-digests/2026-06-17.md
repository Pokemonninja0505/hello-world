## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1 — The Fed regime change is in its early innings, and today's reaction was a non-confirmation.** Markets had two ways to read a hawkish dot plot from a brand-new chair: take the dots at face value (sell duration, buy the dollar, yields up), or bet that Warsh's own communication style overrides the committee's projections (muted reaction). Today, yields eased and vol stayed low — the market chose door two. That's a bet on personality over process, and it is unconfirmed; it gets tested at every Warsh appearance between now and the next meeting. This is early-stage, not crowded — there's no consensus yet on how to trade "Warsh Fed" relative to "Powell Fed."

**Theme 2 — The geopolitical risk premium is unwinding faster than the macro narrative has caught up to.** Oil's -2.56% move on Iran-Israel-US de-escalation news is the same trade that drove gold's pullback from highs. The catch: the inflation print that justified today's hawkish dot plot was explicitly energy-driven. If the June 19 MOU signing holds and oil keeps falling, the Fed's own justification for "no cuts in 2026" erodes within one or two CPI cycles — setting up a potential reversal of today's hawkish repricing later in Q3. This theme is accelerating but carries real binary reversal risk at the June 19 signing date.

## 5. What to Watch Next

1. **Iran-Israel-US MOU signing, June 19** — Bullish: signing proceeds cleanly, Hormuz traffic normalizes, oil extends its decline, broad risk-on. Bearish: signing slips, is contested, or an incident occurs, the Hormuz premium snaps back, oil spikes. Threshold: WTI sustaining below $75 confirms the bullish path; a re-spike above $85 confirms the bearish one.
2. **Chair Warsh's follow-up commentary before the next FOMC** — Bullish: he continues to lean dovish relative to the committee, validating today's muted yield reaction. Bearish: he endorses the committee's hike-risk dots, sending yields and the dollar higher and re-rating growth stocks down. Threshold: any explicit reference to nontrivial 2026 hike odds is the bearish trigger.
3. **July CPI release** — Bullish: headline cools meaningfully back toward 3%, reviving 2026 cut odds and validating the "energy-driven, transitory" read. Bearish: prints at or above 4.2% again, cementing the dot plot's "no cuts" stance. Threshold: a print above 4.3% YoY likely forces a hawkish repricing across the curve.

## 6. Bottom Line

Kevin Warsh's first FOMC decision held rates at 3.50%-3.75% as expected but dropped the 2026 rate cut from the dot plot, triggering a rotation out of tech (Nasdaq -1.15%) into cyclicals (Dow record, +0.64%) rather than a broad selloff, while oil fell 2.56% on Iran-Israel-US de-escalation progress toward a June 19 ceasefire signing. Over the next 3-5 days, expect continued tension between the hawkish dot plot and falling energy prices undercutting the inflation case behind it, with markets watching Warsh's follow-up tone and the MOU signing for confirmation of which force wins. The thesis breaks if the Iran ceasefire collapses or the June 19 signing fails — that would spike oil back up, reignite the inflation argument, validate the hawkish dots, and reverse today's rotation into cyclicals and crypto's risk-off lean alike.
