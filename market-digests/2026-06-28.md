## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1 — The bond market is making an increasingly large bet that oil-driven disinflation overrides the Fed's hawkish dot plot, and that bet just got bigger.** The 10Y has now fallen in three straight sessions (4.50% → 4.45% → 4.37%) even as Warsh's FOMC explicitly signaled a 2026 hike bias and May PCE confirmed inflation is trending up, not down. The justification keeps strengthening on the surface — WTI just posted its lowest close since before the war — but the move happened *despite*, not because of, calm geopolitics: Iran and the US traded strikes on military bases overnight and oil still fell. That means the bond market isn't just pricing oil's current level, it's pricing oil's continued decline *through* an active, unresolved conflict. This is no longer an early-stage divergence; it's a crowded, one-directional bet with very little room for the Gulf situation to deteriorate further before yields would need to snap back violently — which would hit small caps and any rate-sensitive long simultaneously with an energy-short reversal.

**Theme 2 — The AI trade is entering a "show me" phase across three fronts at once.** Memory/component cost inflation is now visibly compressing margins at the consumer-hardware level (Apple, Microsoft), the public-market on-ramp for the largest private AI labs is closing (OpenAI and reportedly Anthropic delaying IPOs, explicitly citing SpaceX's collapse), and a high-profile single-name re-rating (Qualcomm) failed to survive 48 hours without a hard number behind it. None of these alone ends the AI capex story, but together they mark a shift from a market that rewarded narrative and TAM slides to one demanding delivered numbers — Micron cleared that bar this week; Qualcomm did not. Expect continued bifurcation: capital concentrating in names with proof (memory/HBM beats) and punishing names running on investor-day promises.
