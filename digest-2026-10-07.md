## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The AI Earnings Thesis vs. the Bond Market Math**

The market is in an unusual state where record equity prices coexist with 5.3% 10Y yields — a combination that historically implies one of them is wrong. The resolution mechanism is earnings: if Q3 EPS comes in at ~29.5% growth (as Goldman forecasts), the equity premium against bonds can be partially justified. But this earnings cycle is extraordinarily concentrated — Nvidia and Micron drive one-third of it. A disappointment from either, or any signal that AI capex is decelerating, would pull the equity story apart at the seams while leaving yields elevated. We are in the late innings of this "dual tolerance" — equities tolerating high yields because earnings are growing fast enough to compensate. The window where this works narrows as yields stay high. This is a trade you own until the data breaks it, with Q3 earnings (kicking off mid-October) as the next inflection.

**Theme 2: Hormuz Geopolitical Risk Repricing**

The re-escalation of Iranian tanker attacks is not yet fully priced into equities or credit. Oil at $90 WTI / $102 Brent is elevated, but VLCC rates at $1M/day signal a structural tightening in the physical market that precedes price. The historical pattern in Hormuz escalation cycles is: freight costs and war-risk premiums spike first → physical prices lag by 2–4 weeks → inflation re-acceleration surprises 6–8 weeks later. If this escalation holds or expands, the Fed is caught between a weakening labor market (weak jobs) and re-accelerating goods inflation. That is an explicitly stagflationary input. Energy is the expression — but the second-order trade is watching Fed rate expectations reverse as CPI surprises to the upside in November.
