## 4. Macro & Market Themes

### Theme 1: The AI Capex Reckoning — We Are Not at the Bottom Yet

The market entered 2026 pricing AI infrastructure as a near-term cash flow story. Alphabet's print changed the framework: the companies building the most (MSFT, META, AMZN, GOOG) are now being asked to justify their spending against actual revenue. The "bogey" game — where management guides to a number the market then prices as a floor — is the new earnings dynamic.

We are not in the early innings of this reckoning. Alphabet has already moved. But MSFT, META, AAPL, and AMZN have not. If even two of the four show Alphabet-like capex patterns, the Nasdaq's premium compresses meaningfully. Conversely, one clean MSFT beat with monetization proof could reset the narrative and provide a Nasdaq rally to 26,000+.

The structural issue is that Moody's has now flagged credit quality risk from hyperscaler AI spending — meaning this is no longer just an equity multiple story, it is bleeding into credit. Free cash flow turning negative at Alphabet is the tell. The model was built on expanding cloud margins; if capex is eating all of it, the valuation paradigm shifts.

**Where we are in the trade:** Mid-cycle reckoning. Not early. Not late. Wednesday night is the pivotal moment.

### Theme 2: Iran/Oil as the Macro Strangler

The Iran conflict is creating a cost-push inflation shock at a time when the Fed has no room to cut. Oil at $89–90 with a realistic tail risk of $110+ on full Hormuz closure puts every 2026 rate cut in serious jeopardy. This is the mechanism: oil higher → energy input costs rise → PCE does not fall → Fed holds → real rates stay elevated → equity multiples compress, especially growth/tech.

The Dow's outperformance over Nasdaq is a direct expression of this: energy, defense, and dividend names are either beneficiaries or insulated. But if oil moves above $100 — a real possibility given 13 days of US military strikes with no resolution visible — consumer discretionary implodes, airlines get crushed, retailers see margin compression, and the Q3 earnings outlook deteriorates.

The Fed meeting Wednesday is critical not for the rate decision (hold is certain) but for how Powell frames energy-driven inflation. If he calls it "transient," the market rallies on relief. If he acknowledges it as durable, 10Y breaks 4.60% and equities re-price on Thursday morning — right before AAPL and AMZN print.
