## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. U.S. Military Strikes Iranian Rocket Launchers Near Strait of Hormuz (HIGH IMPACT)**
Over the weekend (Sunday, Aug 30), U.S. forces struck Iranian rocket launchers that were reportedly preparing to deploy mines into the Strait of Hormuz, ending approximately one month of relative calm. The Strait — which transits roughly one-fifth of global oil supplies — had been partially operating under Iran-Oman diplomatic talks. This strike re-escalates the conflict and directly threatens the fragile reopening. Brent surged 2.93% on open. Positioning implication: long energy, long defense, short airlines and rate-sensitive consumer. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi had previously signaled Hormuz would not reopen without U.S. concessions (eased sanctions, war reparations), making near-term resolution unlikely.

**2. Fed Chair Warsh Delivers Hawkish Jackson Hole Speech (HIGH IMPACT)**
At Friday's Jackson Hole conference, Chair Kevin Warsh stated that "while this summer's inflation readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying inflation trends have meaningfully improved," and signaled the Fed has "work to do" if inflation does not move toward the 2% target "clearly and at sufficient speed." September rate hike probability moved to 60.4% per CME FedWatch (up from ~56% prior). Rabobank characterized it as reshaping the rate outlook. Positioning implication: bearish for rate-sensitive growth, small caps, and long-duration assets; dollar strength. The contrast with market's prior expectation of a pause is the shock — this is not a consensus outcome.

**3. Nvidia Blockbuster Earnings Drive Semiconductor Rally (MID-HIGH IMPACT — earlier in week)**

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The Warsh Pivot Reversal — From Pause to Hike**

The market spent most of July and early August pricing a Fed pause or even a rate cut by year-end. That trade was unwound at Jackson Hole. Warsh's speech marks a hawkish pivot that is only beginning to be absorbed. At 60.4% probability of a September hike, the market has not fully capitulated — further pricing of the hike (toward 80%+) would trigger another leg down in rate-sensitive equities: small caps (RUT), long-duration growth (Nasdaq), homebuilders, and utilities. The dollar also hasn't fully reacted yet. This theme has more room to run over the next 3–5 sessions. The crowded position coming in was "soft landing, pause/cut" — that position is now wrong and is still being unwound.

**Theme 2: The Hormuz Premium Is Back — And May Stick**

The Strait of Hormuz conflict has been the defining macro variable of 2026. After briefly calming in late July through August, the weekend re-escalation confirms the situation is not resolved. Brent is now up 33% year-over-year. The oil supply disruption is characterized by the IEA as the largest in global oil market history. The equity market has, so far, processed this as a sector rotation rather than a systemic risk — but at $90+ Brent with Hormuz still unreliable, the stagflationary feedback loop tightens: higher oil = higher inflation = more Fed hikes = more multiple compression. This is the theme that connects both macro threads. The risk is these two headwinds (oil and rates) are not independent — they reinforce each other.
