## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. Anthropic IPO — The AI Valuation Moment (Highest Impact)**

Anthropic (Nasdaq: ANT) priced at $83/share raising ~$81B in the largest IPO by initial deal size on record. It opened on Nasdaq at $111.50 Thursday and closed Day 1 at $97.17 (+17%). The market's response was to sell AI-adjacent equities for a sixth consecutive session. The IPO crystallized a "peak AI valuation" trade: if the most prominent private AI lab carries a $1.76 trillion public market value and can't hold its first-day gains, the market is no longer pricing AI optionality at any price. Friday (Day 2), ANT is sliding toward ~$87–88 (–9%), heading back toward the $83 IPO price. NVDA announced a ~$10B private placement purchase of Anthropic stock — it creates a valuation anchor but hasn't arrested the broader selloff. If ANT closes below $83 in its first week of trading, the symbolic signal for AI sentiment is severe.

**2. FOMC Minutes Confirm December Hike (High Impact)**

Wednesday's FOMC minutes showed "near-unanimous" support for a December 25bp rate hike. Governor Waller explicitly floated 50bp. Market pricing has moved to ~96% probability of a December hike. The minutes confirmed what bond markets feared: the 2026 pause was a pause, not a pivot. The terminal rate question is live again. This is the structural headwind for growth equity multiples. Core PCE on November 25 is now the most important near-term data point.

**3. AMD — Short Seller China Report (High Impact)**

An activist short released a report alleging AMD's China operations carry material, undisclosed exposure. AMD fell 7.72% on Thursday to $489.19; Friday pre-market –6.3%. The China angle adds geopolitical risk premium that is structurally difficult to dismiss or quantify. This is a double hit: sector selloff plus company-specific short pressure. Watch for AMD management response.

**4. Intuit (INTU) — Earnings Miss + Mailchimp Sale (Medium Impact)**

Intuit reported earnings with weaker-than-expected guidance and simultaneously announced the sale of Mailchimp. Thursday close $310.02; Friday pre-market ~$280 (–9.56%). **5. Global Semiconductor Contagion (High Impact)**

Taiwan's Taiex posted its worst week since 1991. TSMC, ASML, and the full Asia chip supply chain sold off in sympathy with US names. The global nature of the move indicates institutional de-risking of AI capex exposure, not just a US repricing. The impact runs from fab (TSMC) to equipment (ASML) to networking (Broadcom) — the entire AI infrastructure chain is being marked down simultaneously.

**6. S&P 500 Intraday Reversal — Technical Bearish Signal (High Impact)**

The S&P 500 was up 1.26% intraday on Thursday before closing down 2.02% — the largest failed intraday reversal since March 20, 2020 (COVID crash). When buyers cannot defend a 1.26% intraday gain and the index closes at session lows, institutional sell programs are dominating every bid. This is not a neutral technical event — it is a capitulation-failure, meaning the bulls tried and lost.

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: AI Valuation Reset — Structural, Not Cyclical**

The Anthropic IPO was supposed to validate the AI investment cycle. It became a "sell the news" event that is exposing how crowded AI positioning had become. The mechanism: Anthropic at $1.76T is the first time the market could price a full AI lab in public market terms — and it rejected that price immediately. With $6 trillion stripped from the S&P 500's market cap over six weeks, and global semiconductor indices posting their worst performances since 2022, this has the profile of a structural repricing rather than a healthy correction pause. The critical marker to watch: does the AI sector find a new equilibrium multiple, or does it continue to unwind toward pre-2024 AI-era valuations? We are not close to that answer yet.

**Theme 2: Fed Re-Engagement — 5% Yield Is Not a Ceiling**

The FOMC minutes were the second major catalyst this week. At 5% on the 10Y, the discount rate pressure on growth equities is acute. If Waller's 50bp case gains traction between now and Core PCE, the terminal rate conversation moves from "5.25%" to "5.50%+" and forces another leg of multiple compression. The dollar near a 19-month high is the companion trade: earnings headwinds for US multinationals, credit stress in emerging markets, and reduced commodity import demand globally. This theme has legs through December FOMC.
