## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The 5% Treasury Ceiling — High-Multiple Equity vs. Risk-Free Rate**
We are in the part of the cycle where the economy is strong enough to prevent a Fed pivot, but strong enough that the market cannot ignore the discount rate. PMI at 57.6 and inflation expectations at 4.7% mean the Fed has no cover to cut and every reason to at least threaten another hike. The 10Y at 5% is not new — it appeared briefly last year — but the difference now is that equity multiples (S&P 500 near 28-30x forward earnings on the Magnificent Seven names) have expanded further, making the multiple compression risk on a yield breakout more severe. We are not in early stages of this trade — the tension between high growth expectations and high rates is the central market conflict of Q4 2026. It resolves one of two ways: tech earnings validate the multiple, or they don't and the air comes out.

**Theme 2: Oil Geopolitical Premium — From Risk Event to Fundamentals**
Oil spent the last two weeks pricing Middle East escalation risk. That risk is now partially deflated by Trump's statement, but the structural picture underneath — OPEC+ supply discipline, US shale response time, and global demand from a 57+ PMI economy — has not changed. The question for energy positioning shifts from "how much risk premium is priced?" to "what is the fundamental equilibrium for Brent?" Most analysts put fair value at $95-105 on fundamentals. $115 reflected geopolitical + fundamental overlay. The unwind to $105-110 would be the mean reversion trade, but it is not a straight line and requires no new escalation.
