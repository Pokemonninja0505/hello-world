## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The AI Trade Is Differentiating**

The era of the blanket AI trade — where all AI-adjacent names rose together — is over. PLTR's 29% gap demonstrates that pure-play AI software with government and enterprise contracts at scale is still being violently re-rated. AMD's 7% drop on a strong beat shows that hardware/chip names need to deliver expanding guidance, not just strong quarters. The market is asking: "Which AI companies are actually converting AI demand into durable revenue streams?" PLTR is passing that test. AMD is not failing it — but it is not passing it convincingly either. This bifurcation will accelerate through the rest of Q2 earnings season. The names to watch are those with direct AI revenue visibility (not just "we sell to AI customers") versus those with hardware cycles that are inherently lumpy.

We are in the middle innings of this differentiation, not the end. Nvidia's next print is the next major verdict. The market has not yet found the peak of AI capex expectations; it is resetting them name-by-name. This creates meaningful opportunities on both sides.

**Theme 2: Hormuz Resolution Is The Hidden Macro Regime Change**

Markets are pricing a deal, but they have not fully rerated for a world where Hormuz is open. Oil at $74 is already reflecting significant optimism, but a confirmed, operational reopening of the strait would cascade through multiple asset classes simultaneously: oil falls further (energy earnings compress, consumer spending recovers), gold softens (geopolitical hedge unwinds), airlines and shipping rerate structurally higher, and the inflation picture improves at the margin (reducing the pressure on the Fed to hold rates higher). The risk is that the deal stalls — Iran's stated precondition (US must meet commitments) introduces political uncertainty. This is not a binary that resolves today. But the direction of travel is clear and it is driving the Dow outperformance over Nasdaq in real time.
