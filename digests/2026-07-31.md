## 4. MACRO & MARKET THEMES

**Theme 1: AI Monetization Has Bifurcated — The Market Is Now Sorting Revenue From Spend**

This week's earnings season delivered the definitive answer to the question that has defined tech investing since 2023: who is actually making money from AI? The answer is now visible. MSFT and AMZN have confirmed AI revenue — Azure +43%, AWS AI workload growth, $678B in contracted backlog, 30M Copilot seats. META has confirmed AI spend without revenue. AAPL has confirmed AI lag — it is not yet a meaningful AI revenue story.

The market is not at the beginning of this trade; it is in the middle. The first leg was "buy all AI exposure" — that trade ran from 2023 through early 2026. The second leg is "buy AI companies that have demonstrated revenue; underweight AI companies that are still spending." We are entering that second leg now. MSFT and AMZN are the clear winners of the first round. The companies that fail to demonstrate AI revenue in the next 2–3 quarters will face multiple compression regardless of top-line growth.

The trade has at least 2–3 more quarters of runway before AI winners and AI pretenders fully converge in price. Do not diversify indiscriminately across tech — the divergence within tech is the alpha.

**Theme 2: The Fed Is Pinned — Stagflation Risk Is the Backdrop for Every Trade**

Q2 GDP at 1.5% with core PCE at 3.3% and the 10Y at 4.67% is not a soft landing. It is mild stagflation. The Fed is in the worst position possible: cutting would be inflationary; hiking into a GDP slowdown risks recession; holding indefinitely with three dissenters wanting hikes signals the committee is losing confidence in the "hold and wait" strategy.

The equity market is ignoring this because AI earnings are so dominant that they are overriding macro headwinds — at least at the mega-cap level. The Russell 2000's underperformance is where the stagflation signal is showing up in prices. Small-cap stocks cannot absorb the macro pressure that mega-cap AI names are insulated from by their earnings growth.

If the next PCE print comes in at 3.4%+ or if Q3 GDP shows continued deceleration, the macro backdrop reasserts itself. The bull case depends on AI earnings growing fast enough to justify multiples that the 10Y at 4.67% mathematically constrains. That math gets harder, not easier, if the yield curve does not cooperate.
