**1. July Nonfarm Payrolls: -23,000 (Expected: +83,000)**
The biggest market-moving event of the week. The economy shed jobs for the first time in the recovery cycle. Unemployment fell slightly to 4.1% from 4.2% estimated, but the headline number dominated. Wage growth slowed to its weakest pace in nearly five years. The immediate market read: the Fed has no justification to hike in September. This single data point repriced the entire September FOMC pricing from 55% to 42% hike probability. Implication: stay long equities and long duration into CPI next week — but the trade is crowded now.

**2. Palantir Q2 Earnings: Revenue +93% YoY, Stock +29.5%**
Reported Monday (Aug 4), but drove sentiment all week. Net income $1.07B ($0.41/share) vs. $329M a year ago. Revenue near-doubled driven by commercial AI sovereign tools. The company raised 2026 revenue guidance to $8.15B–$8.16B with U.S. commercial guidance above $3.424B. The stock's nearly 30% one-day gain reignited AI spending confidence across the sector. Implication: the AI capex story is intact and accelerating into the back half of the year; Applied Materials earnings this week are now a higher-stakes confirmation event.

**3. Iran Formalizes Hormuz Operational Plan, U.S. Rejects It**
Iran published an official operational plan prohibiting U.S. and Israeli vessels from the Strait of Hormuz. Washington rejected the proposal categorically. This is an escalation in framing, not yet in action — the strait remains open. The risk is not today's price but the asymmetric tail: if the situation moves from diplomatic to kinetic, oil re-spikes to $100+ and the equity rally breaks. The market is currently pricing this as non-event, which means it is not hedged. Watch oil and VIX Monday as the first signal on how traders assess the weekend news.

**4. Boeing: FAA Approves 737 Max 7, Analyst Upgrade Cycle**
Boeing gained 4.8% Monday to $226 after BNP Paribas upgraded from Underperform to Outperform. JPMorgan raised its price target to $290. The FAA approval for the 737 Max 7 after years of delays removes a major overhang. Boeing is on track for its best week since early April. The company has multiple simultaneous tailwinds: regulatory normalization, analyst consensus revisions, and commercial aviation cycle recovery. Implication: setup for a multi-month recovery trade if operational execution follows through.

**5. Airbnb Q2 Beat: EPS $1.37 vs. $1.25 Est., Revenue $3.61B vs. $3.58B Est.**
Reported after the close August 6. Beat on both top and bottom line. Middle East travel headwind was flagged in guidance but did not materially impair results. Stock was trading near $152 into the print. Consumer travel spending remains resilient despite broader labor softness. Implication: consumer discretionary is not rolling over — the jobs report hurt payrolls but has not yet hit spending behavior.

**6. ExxonMobil Q2: $14.5B Net Income, $3.48/Share**

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The Jobs-Driven Dovish Pivot — How Far Does It Go?**

The market has now fully pivoted from "how many more hikes" to "how soon does the Fed pause." The July jobs number was not a soft landing — it was an outright decline of 23,000 jobs. This is the first contraction in payrolls during this cycle. Historically, this kind of inflection precedes a shift in Fed tone within 1–2 meetings.

The critical question is whether this is a one-month aberration or the start of a trend. The August jobs report (due early September) will determine that. In the meantime, markets are trading the hope: equities up, yields down, dollar soft, gold up.

Where are we in the trade: the first week of repricing is done. The crowding risk is now real — everyone is positioned for a dovish Fed. If CPI next week prints hot (above 3.0%), the entire thesis gets stress-tested simultaneously. The risk is not gradual — it's binary, which is why VIX at 14.90 is complacent.

**Theme 2: AI Capex Is Now a Confirmed Economic Force, Not a Narrative**

Palantir's 93% revenue growth is not a one-company story — it reflects enterprise adoption of AI tools at a pace that has outrun analyst estimates by a factor of roughly 2x (Q2 earnings growth now tracking 48% vs. 24% estimate at the season's start). This is a genuine acceleration, not multiple expansion.

The implication for markets: AI spending is creating a second order of winners beyond the hyperscalers — software companies converting demand into recurring revenue. The market is in the early innings of repricing this class of beneficiaries. The AMAT earnings this week are the next data point testing whether the infrastructure spend is still accelerating.

## 5. What to Watch Next

**1. CPI — Tuesday, August 12**
- Bullish outcome: CPI prints below 2.8% YoY, confirming the jobs miss was demand-led disinflation. Fed hike odds collapse further, Treasuries rally, equities extend, growth stocks lead.
- Bearish outcome: CPI prints above 3.0% YoY, revealing that inflation is sticky despite soft labor. Fed is trapped — they cannot cut but the economy is weakening. Stagflation narrative re-emerges and the equity rally faces a sharp retest.
- Threshold that matters: 2.8% is the consensus; above 3.0% is the pain trade. Anything below 2.5% is a genuine surprise and likely sends yields sharply lower.

**2. Applied Materials (AMAT) Earnings**
- Bullish outcome: WFE guidance maintained or raised; AI chip demand absorbing capacity; AMAT guides Q4 above consensus. Confirms the semiconductor equipment cycle is intact.
- Bearish outcome: Cautious commentary on China restrictions limiting demand, or customers deferring orders. Semiconductor equipment is a leading indicator — a miss here questions the entire AI capex story with 2–3 quarters of lead time.
- Threshold: Watch WFE revenue guidance vs. Street estimates specifically, not just headline EPS.

**3. Iran / Hormuz — Ongoing**
- Bullish outcome (for equities, not oil): Diplomatic de-escalation, Iran backtracks on the operational plan, oil falls back toward $70 on relief, equity risk premium compresses.
- Bearish outcome: Any reported incident in the strait — a vessel boarding, missile test, or blocked passage — sends WTI back toward $90–$100 and breaks the equity narrative with an inflation shock overlay.
- Threshold: Watch tanker traffic data and any Pentagon communication Monday morning. This is the tail risk the market is not pricing.

**4. Fed Speakers — Hammack and Barkin**
- Bullish: Either signals openness to holding rates through year-end if data remains weak. Markets add to the dovish trade.
- Bearish: Either characterizes July payrolls as noise and reaffirms willingness to hike in September. Fed hike odds spike back toward 55%+ and Treasury yields reverse.
