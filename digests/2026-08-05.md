## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. US-Iran Strait of Hormuz deal — material progress, deal not yet signed**
Qatar delivered an interim proposal. Washington and Tehran both signaled constructive momentum. Trump had previously called off planned military action to give diplomacy a chance. This is the single biggest macro catalyst in the market today. Oil dropped from the mid-$80s earlier this week to $76 on these hopes. The implication is significant: the Strait accounts for roughly 20% of global oil flow. If reopened, the structural supply overhang would pressure energy prices toward the $65-70 range on WTI. Positioning implication: energy sector faces a real ceiling here; airlines, transport, and consumer discretionary have a tailwind if the deal closes. Risk: if talks collapse — which is entirely possible given prior failed rounds in June and July — oil snaps back violently and the equity rally partially reverses.

**2. Arista Networks (ANET) Q2 blowout — first $3B quarter, guidance raised to ~40% annual growth**
Revenue $3.036B (+37.7% YoY), EPS $1.02 vs. consensus $0.89 (15% beat). Full-year revenue outlook raised to approximately $12.6B, implying ~40% growth. Stock jumped approximately 14%. This is not a beat-and-fade setup — this is genuine fundamental acceleration. AI-driven networking demand is not plateauing; it is compounding. Arista's customers (hyperscalers, cloud providers) are accelerating CapEx, and Arista is the primary beneficiary within the networking layer. Positioning implication: ANET is now a momentum name with fundamental backing. The 14% gap is large, but the guidance raise at this scale ($12.6B implies another ~$3B+ quarter average for H2) suggests the move is warranted, not stretched.

**3. Disney Q3 2026 — EPS beat, revenue slight miss, structural narrative intact**
EPS $2.06 vs. $1.86 expected (11% beat); Revenue $25.25B vs. $25.4B expected (slight miss). Experiences segment (parks, cruises) up 10% YoY to $9.97B. Entertainment operating income +64% to $1.7B. Sports (ESPN) operating income -17% to $858M — the one soft spot. Disney sold its 50% A+E Media stake to Hearst for ~$1.2B, which it is using to fund a raised buyback target (at least $9B in FY2026). Stock gained ~4% in premarket. The key read here: Disney's re-rating story (from content company to experiences-plus-streaming platform) is working. The EPS beat at $2.06 is substantially ahead of where consensus was entering fiscal 2026. Positioning implication: DIS is in the early innings of margin expansion from streaming maturity and parks pricing power. Not a trade for fast money — a hold for patient capital.

**4. Eli Lilly (LLY) Q2 results — reporting today, consensus $8.84 EPS on $20.44B revenue**
This is the GLP-1 read. Street expects $20.44B, up from $15.56B a year ago — a 31% YoY growth expectation embedded in consensus. Any miss on Zepbound/Mounjaro volumes or a lowered production guidance would be punished. Any beat on volumes with raised FY guidance would push the stock to new highs. The options market priced approximately $65-76B in potential swing — binary in both directions. Results were due before the opening bell today; by market close, this was a known outcome. (See Stocks in Focus for setup framing.)

**5. AppLovin (APP) Q2 results — reporting after the bell, consensus $3.75 EPS on $1.94B revenue**
AppLovin has been one of the highest-momentum AI-adjacent names in 2026. Consensus implies 54% revenue growth YoY ($1.26B a year ago). If it matches or beats, the setup is a continuation of the AI ad-tech re-rating. If it misses, the multiple compression would be sharp given where the stock has likely run. Binary event post-close.

**6. Friday NFP preview — 120K expected, unemployment tick to 4.3%**
June came in at 57K (well below 110K consensus, explained largely by a 61K decline in leisure/hospitality related to World Cup scheduling). July consensus is 120K, which is a rebound driven primarily by leisure/hospitality normalization. Unemployment expected to tick from 4.2% to 4.3%. A print at or below June's 57K would raise genuine labor market concern; a beat above 150K would be read as re-acceleration and would pressure the long end of the Treasury curve.

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The "Goldilocks Reopening" trade — oil falling, growth commodities rising, AI compounding**
The current macro configuration is structurally favorable: energy prices declining (disinflation, consumer relief), copper and industrial metals rising (growth acceleration, supply constraints), AI infrastructure spending compounding quarter over quarter (Arista just guided to 40% annual growth). The 10Y Treasury yield falling alongside equity records means the market is not pricing rate hike risk — it is pricing a soft landing with upside. This combination — copper up, oil down, yields flat-to-down, tech earnings beating — has historically been associated with the most sustained equity bull runs.

Where we are in the trade: Not early. The S&P at 7,736 is pricing a great deal of good news. The AI CapEx cycle is visible to everyone; the Iran deal would be an additional catalyst, not a surprise catalyst. The crowding risk is real — everyone is long tech, long AI names, and short energy. The trade becomes dangerous when the consensus is unanimous, and it is approaching that.

What changes the regime: A failed Iran deal + oil snapping back to $85+ simultaneously with a weak NFP print would be the combination that breaks the Goldilocks narrative. Growth slowing AND energy-driven inflation returning is stagflationary — the worst outcome for equities.

**Theme 2: AI infrastructure spending is structural, not cyclical — the networking layer is the proof**
Arista's first $3 billion quarter is the data point that settles the debate about whether hyperscaler AI CapEx is sustainable or a bubble. A cloud-networking company delivering 37.7% revenue growth and guiding to 40% annual growth is evidence that the data center build-out has accelerating momentum, not decelerating. The key implication: companies in the picks-and-shovels layer of AI infrastructure (networking, cooling, power, storage) are in the early-to-middle innings of a multi-year CapEx supercycle. The risk to this thesis is not demand — it is execution: supply chain constraints, power grid limitations, or a regulatory action against hyperscalers.

## 7. Catalyst Plays

**Catalyst Play 1: Iran deal thesis — oil price collapse beneficiary**
- Theme: A US-Iran framework agreement restoring Strait of Hormuz shipping would remove roughly a $10-15/bbl geopolitical risk premium from WTI, targeting $65-70 on a full deal.
- Why it creates edge now: The interim proposal stage typically precedes a signed agreement by 1-3 weeks. Oil has already moved from $84 to $76 in anticipation. A signed deal would likely produce a final leg down of $6-10/bbl from current levels.
- How to express: Long JETS (airline ETF, benefits from lower jet fuel costs), long AWAY (travel ETF), short XLE (energy sector ETF) or long SCO (2x inverse oil ETF). Airlines have the most direct operational leverage to a sustained fall in oil toward $70.
- Timeframe: 2-4 weeks (deal announcement is the catalyst; airline sector re-rates quickly after).
- Kill thesis: Iran talks collapse for a third consecutive time, or new Hormuz incident. Oil reverses above $82. Exit immediately. Also killed if U.S. imposes new tariffs on Iranian goods that create a fresh supply disruption.

**Catalyst Play 2: AI networking infrastructure supercycle — picks-and-shovels plays**
- Theme: Arista delivering its first $3B quarter and guiding to 40% annual growth confirms that hyperscaler AI CapEx is compounding, not plateauing. The networking layer is the most supply-constrained component of AI data centers — switches, routers, and the software that manages them cannot be fabricated in months.
- Why it creates edge now: ANET's beat and raise proved the demand is real. Analysts are being forced to revise estimates across the sector. Institutional funds underweight networking (relative to semiconductors) will be adding on any pullback.
- How to express: ANET (direct), or SMH (semiconductor broad basket as an alternative if ANET premium is too extended). For the infrastructure layer specifically: data center REITs (EQIX, DLR) also benefit from the continued CapEx cycle.
- Timeframe: 6-12 weeks (this is a quarter-to-quarter trend, not a day trade).
- Kill thesis: A hyperscaler (Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Meta) announces CapEx reduction or AI build-out pause. Or a regulation/antitrust action targeting the cloud providers. Neither is visible today, but both would be sudden when they come.
