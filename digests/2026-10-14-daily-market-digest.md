## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. September CPI: 3.7% YoY — Hotter Than Expected (Impact: High)**
Headline CPI came in at +0.6% MoM and +3.7% YoY, above the 3.6% consensus. Core (ex-food, energy) was +0.3% MoM and +2.6% YoY versus estimates of +0.2% and +2.5%. Gasoline drove it — up 9.1% on the month, accounting for more than half the monthly gain, with the energy index up 5.2% due in part to Houthi-driven supply disruption risk. Shelter rose 0.4% MoM.

*Why it matters:* This is the data point the Fed needed to justify at minimum one more hike in 2026, and potentially opens the door to a 2027 discussion. NY Fed's Williams said yesterday he still sees "one final" 2026 hike — that was before this print. Chair Warsh's mandate ("inflation is too high and has been for too long") is being tested. Stock futures turned negative at 8:30 a.m. ET; Treasury yields shot higher. This print does real damage to the "soft landing is locked in" narrative.

*Positioning implication:* Short duration exposure makes sense. Defensive sectors (utilities, REITs) face multiple compression from rising real yields. Energy continues to benefit from both CPI composition and supply disruption. Tech at 22x+ forward earnings is vulnerable to a further yield reprice.

**2. Houthis Strike Aramco Facility Near Riyadh — Oil Supply Risk Escalates (Impact: High)**
On October 3–4, Houthi forces claimed a successful missile-and-drone strike on Aramco infrastructure south of Riyadh. Satellite imagery confirmed black smoke and burning storage tanks. Saudi authorities did not confirm damage. Brent crude topped $106/barrel in immediate aftermath; it remains elevated.

*Why it matters:* This is not a routine Houthi claim — fires were visually confirmed. Any actual disruption to Saudi refining capacity directly feeds the gasoline channel that drove the September CPI print. Escalation risk is asymmetric: a second, more successful strike could take meaningful barrels offline. Geopolitical risk premium in oil is now a structural CPI input, not a one-off.

*Positioning implication:* Long energy (XLE, OIH) and energy-related inflation hedges. Oil inverse plays only make sense if Hormuz de-escalation is confirmed, which is not on the table.

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: Sticky Inflation Meets a Ceiling on Rate Cuts — The Soft Landing Narrative Is Cracking**

For most of 2026, the consensus was that inflation was coming down fast enough for the Fed to pivot to cuts by mid-2027. That narrative rested on energy prices normalizing and shelter disinflation continuing. September CPI broke both legs: energy spiked on Houthi supply disruption, and shelter rose 0.4% MoM. A 3.7% headline with a 2.6% core is not a central bank that can pivot. Williams' pre-CPI language about "one final hike" now sounds like the floor, not the ceiling. We are early in the realization phase — the market is still priced for a soft landing (S&P at 7,800, VIX at 15) while the data is describing something harder. The gap between price and data is where the trade lives.

**Theme 2: Semiconductor Equipment Cycle Inflection — AI Infrastructure Capex Has a Ceiling**

ASML's failed rally is more important than the number. Equipment companies trade on order book duration, not quarterly results. A beat-and-raise that gets sold means institutional money is moving forward in time and not liking what it sees. CXMT's competitive road map, Chinese memory cost deflation, and potential capex discipline from hyperscalers (who are watching energy costs and rate-sensitive capital budgets) all converge on the same question: is the AI infrastructure capex cycle sustainable at this rate? This is not the end of AI — but it may be the beginning of a mean-reversion in semiconductor equipment multiples. The trade has been crowded for 18 months.
