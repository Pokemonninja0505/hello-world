## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. New US AI Chip Export Restrictions — Day's Dominant Macro Catalyst**
*(Market impact: highest)*

The Trump administration's Commerce Department issued an interim final rule (effective November 16) requiring export licenses for advanced AI chips used in overseas data centers that provide compute access to Chinese-headquartered companies. The rule directly targets the **remote-access loophole** — not merely physical chip shipments. It additionally designates Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand, and Brazil as elevated-scrutiny destinations due to diversion risk. The Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule from January 2025 is simultaneously rescinded.

This is structurally negative for the entire US semiconductor supply chain. Intel and Micron each fell more than 7%; SOX fell 5.3% the day the rule published. The rule permanently compresses the Chinese-adjacent revenue ceiling for Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and Micron — not as a one-time shock but as an ongoing policy constraint. It simultaneously accelerates the Chinese domestic semiconductor buildout (SMIC, Huawei Ascend).

*Positioning implication:* Structurally reduces the revenue ceiling for NVDA, AMD, MU, INTC. Near-term bearish for SOX. Inverse semiconductor exposure (SOXS) has positive asymmetry if restrictions tighten further or enforcement accelerates.

The first earnings report under interim CEO Colette Kress since Jensen Huang resigned November 12 following a scandal involving a suspected Chinese intelligence-linked extramarital affair. Huang's departure came with no named successor; the board is running an external and internal search. Options markets implied a ±10.6% move heading into the print.

Street consensus: ~$108.8B revenue, ~$2.47 EPS. Company guidance: $108B ±2%, 74% gross margin ±50bps. Prior quarter: $96.2B revenue (+106% YoY). The print is almost certainly in-line or above — the question is what guidance under new management signals about forward visibility and China-exposure impact.

*Positioning implication:* The post-print direction defines the medium-term technical setup for the entire sector. Hold off on directional semiconductor trades until result is confirmed.

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The Fed Has Broken the AI Sector Premium**

The AI bull thesis 2024–2025 rested on two pillars: accelerating AI capex (keeping NVDA/AMD growing) and low real rates (justifying elevated multiples). Both are now deteriorating simultaneously. The 10-year at 5.1% compresses the multiple; chip export restrictions compress the revenue ceiling; the leadership vacuum at Nvidia introduces an execution-risk discount not present before November 12. The market is on day three of repricing that combination.

This is not a one-day event. At 18.3x forward earnings with a 40bps equity risk premium, the cushion is structural, not tactical — and it tightens with each additional hike. The overcrowded position is long large-cap AI/tech. The unwind is early-to-mid stage, not exhausted.

**Theme 2: Anthropic IPO as AI Confidence Stress Test**

After 18 months as the most anticipated tech IPO, Anthropic arrives in the worst tape. A $1.4T valuation in a market just reminded that AI infrastructure is not exempt from geopolitical risk (chip curbs) or governance failure (Jensen Huang) is a stress test at the wrong moment. The IPO will either confirm that institutional AI conviction is durable (bullish re-entry signal) or reveal that the appetite was price-dependent (bearish overhang for the whole sector). The pricing Wednesday is as important as NVDA's earnings tonight for calibrating the AI sector's near-term floor.
