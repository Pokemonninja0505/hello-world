## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. Nvidia board opens independent probe into Jensen Huang — NVDA down ~5% premarket**
The board announced Sunday night it is launching an independent investigation after reports surfaced that Huang was romantically involved with an individual identified by intelligence agencies as a Chinese spy. Huang has acknowledged the affair and apologized. The board has stated there is no current evidence that national security or Nvidia's proprietary information was compromised, and Huang remains CEO. A committee of independent directors with outside counsel is overseeing the review. Nvidia reports earnings November 17.

*Why it matters:* The stock was already down 22% from its record before Sunday. This adds an idiosyncratic governance/leadership risk on top of an already-weakened technical picture. The binary on November 17 earnings becomes much harder to hold into — the market now has to price both the results AND the probability that Huang exits. Analyst Dan Ives has flagged removal risk as real. Positioning implication: avoid new longs here ahead of earnings; if short, the thesis has two legs now (AI capex concerns + leadership uncertainty).

**2. Thursday's AI/data center selloff — the anatomy of a 4.4% S&P 500 decline**
Last Thursday, the S&P fell 4.4% and the Nasdaq fell 5.5% — the worst single-day losses since April 2025. The trigger was a combination of post-midterm policy fears (Democratic gains raising questions about data-center buildout pace and regulatory intensity on big tech) and reports of deteriorating capex guidance in the hyperscaler supply chain. The selloff was broad but concentrated in AI-exposed names.

*Why it matters:* Thursday's move reset the technical landscape for the entire AI trade. The Nasdaq is now in correction. Friday's bounce was partial and volume-thin. Monday's stabilization in futures is not a reversal signal — it is a dead-cat setup until proven otherwise by either CPI relief or a credible earnings catalyst.

**3. October CPI due Tuesday — December Fed hike at 75–80% odds**
October's jobs report (+165K, in line) came in solid enough to cement December hike expectations. Tomorrow's CPI is now the pivotal event. Deutsche Bank forecasts +0.20% MoM headline. ING says +0.3% would be sufficient to lock in the December hike. Citi projects a 25bps hike in December, then a hold through H1 2027 before cuts begin.

*Why it matters:* The market is essentially a binary going into tomorrow. A 0.2% or softer print could allow the 10Y yield to retreat toward 4.7% and give growth stocks a relief window. A 0.3%+ print pushes the 10Y toward 5%, pressures PE multiples further, and likely retests Thursday's lows. This is the single most important scheduled data point of the week.

**4. Strong October NFP (+165K) already in the rearview but setting context**
The jobs number was not a shock but was solid enough to confirm the Fed's "higher for longer" posture heading into year-end. Combined with still-elevated September CPI (3.7% YoY), there is no data basis for the Fed to signal a pause.

*Why it matters:* Reinforces the rates-hostile environment for high-multiple tech. The NFP number is already priced; CPI tomorrow is the marginal risk.

**5. Midterm election results — Democratic gains create policy overhang for AI/tech**
Democrats won the midterms last week. Market interpretation is that this creates friction for large-scale AI infrastructure spending (data center approvals, power grid permitting, potential antitrust acceleration). The Thursday selloff was partly attributed to this read.

*Why it matters:* This is a multi-month positioning story, not a one-day trade. The AI capex cycle does not stop, but the risk premium on AI infrastructure names has structurally risen.

**6. Bitcoin ETF outflows — $1.28B through November 6**
Spot Bitcoin ETFs have seen sustained outflows as risk-off sentiment hits crypto. BTC trading near $74K, below key support levels, with Fear & Greed at 29. Ethereum fell ~5% Sunday.

*Why it matters:* Crypto is not behaving as a macro hedge here — it is tracking risk assets lower. ETF flow data suggests institutional holders are reducing exposure, not buying the dip.

**7. Monday.com (MNDY) earnings — guided to 16–17% growth in Q3**
MNDY reported or is reporting today. Growth guidance of 16–17% is a deceleration from prior quarters. 

*Why it matters:* In a rising-rate environment, enterprise SaaS multiples are under pressure. Any guide-down from MNDY will add to SaaS sector selling.

**8. Constellation Energy (CEG) earnings today**

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: The AI capex trade is not dead but it is repricing**
The Thursday selloff was the first genuine structural challenge to the AI infrastructure trade since it began. The move was not random — it had two catalysts (midterm results + supply-chain guidance fears) arriving simultaneously. The trade is not over: data center buildout remains a multi-year trend. But the market has begun pricing in execution risk, political risk, and valuation compression from higher rates all at once. We are in the middle of this repricing, not at the end. The catalyst that could restart the trade cleanly is a strong Nvidia earnings on November 17 combined with Huang remaining in his role. Until then, the burden of proof has shifted to bulls.

**Theme 2: The Fed's return to hiking is the compression engine**
The rate cut cycle that the market was pricing in Q1 2026 is fully reversed. We are now back to a hiking environment with December nearly certain. This is not a rotation — it is a multiple compression event for every high-PE growth name. The 10Y at 4.86% is already doing damage; at 5%+ it becomes acute. Gold and energy are the natural beneficiaries of this environment. The catalyst that breaks this theme: a CPI miss (0.2% or below) tomorrow that forces markets to revisit December hike pricing.
