## 2. Top News & Catalysts

**1. Trump Tariffs 10–12.5% on 60 Countries — Effective Midnight Friday (HIGH IMPACT)**

New tariffs took effect at 12:01 AM ET Friday under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974, covering imports from 60 countries that allegedly failed to enforce forced-labor import bans. Affected partners include Canada, Mexico, the UK, India, and the EU — covering 99.4% of US imports by volume. The tariffs replace expiring Section 122 surcharges under a new legal mechanism, effectively re-imposing the broad tariff regime.

*Why it matters:* Broad-based input cost increase, directly inflationary. Amplifies the September rate hike case. Consumer goods and import-heavy retailers face margin pressure. Domestically-oriented small caps face less direct exposure, which partly explains the Russell 2000's YTD lead over the S&P.

*Positioning implication:* Underweight importers and retail. Small-cap domestic bias is structurally supported. Watch for Q3 guidance revisions from companies with large imported goods exposure.

## 4. Macro & Market Themes

**Theme 1: AI Capex Credibility Crisis — Early Innings**

The week's dominant narrative is not "AI is dying." It is that AI spending is now outpacing visible monetization proof, and the market has started demanding evidence. Alphabet raised capex to $195–$205B. Tesla's AI opex surged 47%. But Intel's Data Center AI revenue grew 59% and SMCI is taking $60B in orders. The distinction that matters: Intel and SMCI are infrastructure suppliers being paid for the buildout. Alphabet, Tesla, and by extension Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon are buyers whose capex is not yet clearly converting to margin expansion in the income statement.

We are in the early innings of this bifurcation. The next two weeks of mega-cap earnings (Microsoft, Amazon, Meta) will either confirm this as a sector-wide credibility crisis or reveal it as company-specific Alphabet/Tesla execution problems. If Microsoft Azure grows >38% and capex is stable, Thursday's selloff looks like a pair trade opportunity. If Microsoft's capex also jumps, the entire AI buyer complex gets re-rated lower.

**Theme 2: Stagflation Architecture Is Assembling**

Oil at $100 on Iran conflict. Copper falling 2.7% on demand softness. Tariffs on 99.4% of US imports. CPI at 3.5%, core PCE above 3%. Fed at 3.50–3.75% with a 33% probability of hiking in two days and an 80% probability of hiking in two months. Russell 2000 outperforming S&P 500 by 12 percentage points YTD.

This is not a confirmed stagflation trade yet. But every piece of the architecture is being installed: supply-driven energy inflation, tariff-driven goods inflation, demand-side softening (copper), and a Fed that has signaled it will respond to inflation data rather than anticipate relief. The tell will be the next CPI print: if core stays above 3.5% with oil elevated, the "transitory geopolitical spike" narrative collapses and the re-inflation thesis takes hold. If that happens, the correct macro trade is short long-duration assets, long commodities and energy, and short rate-sensitive equity sectors — a significant rotation from where money has been parked.
