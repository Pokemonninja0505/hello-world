Fed Hikes 25bps to 3.75%–4.00% — First in Three Years, Warsh Hawkish
The FOMC voted 12-0 on September 16 to raise the fed funds rate to a 3.75%–4.00% target range, the first hike since 2023. Chair Warsh stated "inflation is too high and has been for too long" and "this summer's readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved." The dot plot signals another hike before year-end, with eight officials projecting a further move. The Dow dropped 631 points on hike day before recovering through Friday. The market's recovery was not a pivot signal — it was short covering off an oversold condition.
POSITIONING: Underweight rate-sensitive (utilities, REITs, long-duration). Favor shorter duration. Higher-for-longer is now the base case, not the tail risk.

Bank of Japan Hikes to 1.25% — Highest Rate Since 1995

The Fed and BOJ tightening in the same week is structurally significant: the yen carry trade, which funded risk positions across emerging markets and crypto for two years, is now mathematically deteriorating. POSITIONING: Watch USD/JPY for forced carry unwind. Long FXY or short USD/JPY. Risk-off cascade possible if yen strengthens sharply.

Macro & Market Themes
Theme 1: Synchronized Global Tightening — The Carry Regime is Over
For two years, the yen carry trade — borrowing cheap yen to fund positions in US equities, EM debt, and risk assets — was the liquidity engine that explained much of the rally in risk. The BOJ was the last major central bank holding near-zero rates. That engine is now running in reverse: BOJ at 1.25% with more hikes signaled, Fed resuming its own cycle. This is the first synchronized global tightening since the 2022-2023 period that preceded the last correction. The market is not pricing this yet — VIX at 14.81 during a week where two G7 central banks hiked tells you that. We are in the early innings of a regime change. The question is whether the unwinding is orderly (gradual yen appreciation, slow repricing) or disorderly (August 2024-style flash crash if a large carry position unwinds suddenly). The current setup — markets making new highs, VIX compressed, 10Y above 5% — rhymes with late 2022 before the next leg down.
Theme 2: Iran War Oil Premium — The Floor, Not the Peak
The consensus trade after the pipeline restart announcement is that WTI at $100 is a ceiling and the market normalizes lower. This is the wrong read. The structural damage to Middle East supply infrastructure — Saudi pipeline at half capacity for weeks, Houthi control of Bab al-Mandeb, Iran weighing NPT exit — represents a multi-month risk premium, not a single-event spike. Q2 earnings estimates in energy were built assuming $85-90 WTI; at $100+, they'll be revised up sharply. The Fed's BOJ-forced admission that inflation is "too high" is also an indirect oil story — higher oil feeds CPI which feeds more hikes which feeds the carry unwind. These are all the same trade, linked. Copper at $6.62 (+44.66% YoY) signals the demand side is still intact despite all of this, which means the market is not in a demand-destruction regime — yet. That distinction matters for timing exits.
